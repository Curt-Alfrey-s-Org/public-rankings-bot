# Fargo Rating Scale Interpretation

**Source:** Fargo Rate documentation and community consensus.  
**Reference:** [FargoRate.com](https://www.fargorate.com/)

---

## Rating Bands (What Numbers Mean)

| Band | Rating | Typical Level | Notes |
|------|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Elite** | 800+ | Top professionals | World-class; consistent runouts |
| **Top pro** | 750–799 | Professional open | Tour regulars; high finish rate |
| **Pro / Open** | 700–749 | Strong amateur / semi-pro | Competitive in open events |
| **Majority** | 200–700 | League and regional | Most players fall in this range |
| **Developing** | under 200 | Beginner / casual | Few games; rating emerging |

## Global Coupling Benefit

Fargo uses **global coupling**: indirect match connections across regions create a unified rating scale. A 600 in Texas and a 600 in Germany represent the same skill level. Same rating = same skill across regions.

---

## Robustness (Games Count)

- **Low games** (under 200): Rating is less stable; starter/prior classification influences result.
- **200+ games**: Rating converges; starter influence decays toward zero.
- **High robustness**: More confidence in the number; less variance from new matches.

---

## 7-Ft Table Context

On 7-ft tables, runout rates and context differ from 9-ft. A 450-rated player's runout rate in league data may differ from 9-ft tournament play. Use table-size context when interpreting ratings for specific formats.
